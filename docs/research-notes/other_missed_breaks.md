# How much could the other missed breaks move ROC-AUC?

On the primary local partition, **0.87911421 to 0.880** requires **127,554 additional correctly ordered positive–negative pairs** out of 144 million. At 20% false positives the model catches 9,410 of 12,000 genuine breaks and misses 2,590. Of those misses, **1,857 are outside the original group of 821**. Passing about 69 additional negatives per one of those 1,857 would close the gap if all other pair orderings held steady. These are required changes in rank, not a forecast. ### What happened when we targeted series with serial dependence

A fixed 25% increase in the score change from the locked baseline to the latest ensemble was applied only to series with pre-boundary serial dependence. On the 643, AUC rose from **0.577367 to 0.588281**, and recovery at 20% false positives rose from **205 to 216**. AUC on the 821 also rose, from 0.498719 to 0.500186, while the overall result **fell to 0.878993**. The gain among these difficult cases was outweighed by losses on the other positives against the same negatives. A smaller change confined to the final output layer gained about 0.000007 overall on the primary partition and lost on the additional partition. Neither edit replaced the weights. [Fixed weight edits](../../research/pipeline_dev/legacy_cpu/experiments/weight_space/other_dynamics_weights.py) · [643-case improvement and full-cohort cost](../tune_out/weight_space/local_rank_dynamics_serial_physics.json)

Claude found that the phase evidence contributed most strongly to its positive-autocorrelation family. In the negative-autocorrelation family the strongest contributions came from changes in the noise and volatility. A separate check compared 643 historical misses to 643 distinct no-break controls from the same pre-boundary dynamics families. We tested whether dependence changes at longer lags while dependence between adjacent points stays fixed. The mean difference in evidence was −0.030 with a paired 95% interval of −0.073 to +0.013. Including the new measurements gave 0.879104 on the primary partition. ### What the other detectors saw

A later [trial on serially dependent series](../../research/weight_candidates/quiet_serial_trial/README.md) gained **7,993 rankings on the 1,857 other current misses**. Across the whole cohort it gained **8,604**, reaching **0.879174**. It improved six of seven output partitions and lost 0.000040 on the seventh comparison by seed 29. At 20% false positives it recovered one additional break. [Full pair accounting](../tune_out/weight_space/other_recovery_pair_accounting.csv)

Six previously trained detector families each used their own 20% false-positive cutoff. At least one flagged **598 of the 1,857** other current misses, alongside 1,351 additional false alarms. At least three flagged **237** and 485 additional false alarms. These are opportunities to distinguish a good disagreement from a bad one. Lowering the ensemble threshold to include all disagreements would change the false-positive rate. [Individual detectors](../tune_out/weight_space/other_miss_witnesses_individual.csv) · [Agreement counts](../tune_out/weight_space/other_miss_witnesses_agreement.csv)

Each of the 598 was matched to a distinct no-break series with similar ensemble rank, detector votes, segment lengths and pre-boundary dynamics. In 98.5% of pairs the same number of detectors called a break. We used 299 pairs to select one measurement and its direction in each of 12 families and reserved the other 299 for confirmation. One measurement passed the multiple-comparison correction. The existing memory model's `unit_start` score ranked the genuine break higher in **57.2%** of confirmation pairs (Holm-adjusted p = 0.0063). It also ranked the genuine break higher in **53.3%** of another **1,259 disjoint pairs** in which none of the six detectors called a break (one-sided p = 0.0154). [Matching](../tune_out/weight_space/other_disagreement_matching.json) · [All 12 comparisons](../tune_out/weight_space/other_disagreement_replication.csv) · [Disjoint comparison](../tune_out/weight_space/other_unit_disjoint_replication.json)

The archived score allows a little probability for every possible slope before the boundary. When we removed that allowance and scored only random-walk-like persistence, the paired win rate fell from **57.2% to 51.2%**. The exact persistent component supplied most of the original likelihood in just **77 of 299** breaks and **71 of 299** controls. Allowing persistence to change after the boundary scored 47.2% against keeping it fixed on both sides. Claude separately found that an observed drop in persistence among 1,846 random-walk-like series had AUC 0.4974. Reweighting the original measurement produced **0.879112** overall, versus 0.879114. [Component reconstruction](../../research/pipeline_dev/legacy_cpu/experiments/weight_space/other_strict_memory.py) · [Paired component results](../tune_out/weight_space/strict_unit_memory_disagreements.json) · [Weight trial](../tune_out/weight_space/local_rank_persistent_pre_dynamics.json)

Five older feature-tree models flagged **808 of the 1,857**, including **428 of the 1,259** missed by all six recent detector families. In the same previously unflagged group they raised **1,182** more false alarms. All five agreed on 46 of those 428 breaks and 96 of those 1,182 controls. Adding the old votes as a separate correction yielded **0.879057** overall. Fitting them jointly with the physical detector votes yielded **0.879028**. Both fell below the saved weights. For the 1,259 breaks that none of the six recent detectors flagged, a twelve-family discovery and confirmation screen found no measurement that passed the multiple-comparison correction. A separate test of how freely the slope could vary before versus after the boundary ranked the break higher in 57.9% of the original confirmation pairs and 49.9% of the 1,259 disjoint pairs. [Screen](../tune_out/weight_space/other_no_detector_replication.csv) · [Slope comparison](../tune_out/weight_space/other_slope_asymmetry.json)

One new measure counted whether short sequences of moves had more similar neighbors on their own side of the boundary than on the opposite side. It ranked the break higher in **674 of the 1,259 pairs** (53.5%) where no recent detector flagged a break, and **280 of 598** (46.8%) where a detector did. The fixed measure used 12 neighbors and required agreement across separate blocks of observations. Across all 24,000 series, a broader neighborhood correction scored **0.878829**. A correction that could change only scores below the training-fold cutoff scored **0.879102**. All these comparisons reuse the same labelled population. [Paired counts](../tune_out/weight_space/other_neighbor_pairs.json) · [Full-cohort results](../tune_out/weight_space/local_rank_transition_neighbors.json) · [Below-cutoff correction](../tune_out/weight_space/local_rank_below_cutoff.json)

We next tested whether the change was stronger at the supplied boundary than at other times in the same raw series. This ranked the break higher in **659 of 1,259** no-flag pairs and **292 of 598** pairs with a flag. Adding the timing measurements to the ensemble gave **0.879014** on the primary partition. Claude added both the neighborhood and the timing measurements to its complete original-view tree, training the baseline and extended versions on the same sources. ROC-AUC fell from **0.87324 to 0.87266**, with losses on four of five folds.
