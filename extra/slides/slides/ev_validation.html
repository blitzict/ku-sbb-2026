How I tested it

Held out whole, fitted on training only

Whole series

The 24,000 series go into 5 groups of 4,800. A held-out series is held out whole: none of its points is ever trained on. That copies the real test, which is 4,000 brand-new series.

Training only

In each round, picking the top 100 columns, fitting the 36 noise priors and training all 3 models use the 4 training groups only. Tree counts are fixed, so the held-out group isn't even used to decide when to stop.

No labels inside

Every measurement is computed from the series itself, without labels. 2 tables that were fitted on all the labels are left out.

A leak I caught

0.8774

Picking the columns once, with every round’s labels, scored 0.8774. Too good to be true. Picked inside each round, the honest score is 0.873.

Did I fool myself?

Held-out: 0.8723. Kaggle, 4,000 unseen series: 0.8758. No.

Folds: try4 uses the seed-7 folds stored in tune_out/weight_space/law_outer7/fold*_rows.npz, the same folds the noise-law priors were fitted on. Each fold’s prior is fitted on that fold’s training labels only (law_outer7), feature ranking is LightGBM gain on the fold’s training rows only, and the models use fixed rounds (LightGBM 720 and 1,300, XGBoost 1,100), so there is no early stopping on the held-out rows. Excluded tables: transition_odds and transport_state (priors fitted on all training labels). Why not time-ordered splits: each series is one separate example with one label, the model always sees the whole series, and the test set is new series, not the future of old ones. So the right unit to hold out is the whole series. The leak: a late experiment used the union of the 5 folds’ selected columns (192); it scored 0.8774, versus 0.873 with each fold’s own 100. The union lets a fold’s own labels choose its columns, so it was dropped. Held-out pooled AUC 0.8723; Kaggle public 0.87583.
